
# Adverbs of Frequency: English Grammar Guide

Adverbs of frequency describe how often an action happens. They are commonly used with the present simple tense to talk about daily routines, habits, and regular activities.

This resource explains the most common adverbs of frequency, their positions in sentences, and how to use them correctly. It is suitable for beginner to elementary English learners (A1–A2).

## 1. What Are Adverbs of Frequency?

Adverbs of frequency tell us how often something happens.

Here are the most common adverbs of frequency, from the highest frequency to the lowest:

| Adverb | Approximate Frequency | Example |
|---|---:|---|
| Always | 100% | I always brush my teeth. |
| Usually | About 80–90% | She usually walks to school. |
| Normally | About 80–90% | We normally have dinner at 7 p.m. |
| Often | About 60–70% | He often plays football. |
| Sometimes | About 40–50% | They sometimes watch movies. |
| Occasionally | About 20–30% | I occasionally eat fast food. |
| Rarely | About 5–10% | She rarely drinks coffee. |
| Seldom | About 5–10% | He seldom goes to the cinema. |
| Hardly ever | Almost 0% | We hardly ever eat out. |
| Never | 0% | I never smoke. |

**Note:** These percentages are approximate examples, not fixed grammatical rules. Actual frequency depends on the speaker and situation.

## 2. Position of Adverbs of Frequency

The position of an adverb of frequency depends on the verb in the sentence.

### A. Before Main Verbs

Adverbs of frequency usually come before the main verb.

Examples:

- I always get up early.
- She usually takes the bus to work.
- They often play basketball after school.
- He never eats breakfast.

### B. After the Verb "To Be"

When a sentence uses the verb "to be" (am, is, are), the adverb of frequency usually comes after it.

Examples:

- I am always happy.
- She is usually busy in the morning.
- They are often late.
- He is never rude.

### C. After the First Auxiliary Verb

When a sentence has an auxiliary verb, the adverb of frequency usually comes after the first auxiliary verb.

Examples:

- She can always help you.
- I have never visited Japan.
- They don't usually eat lunch at home.
- He doesn't often watch television.

### D. At the Beginning or End of a Sentence

Some adverbs, especially **sometimes**, **usually**, and **occasionally**, can also appear at the beginning or end of a sentence for emphasis or a more natural conversational style.

Examples:

- Sometimes, I walk to school.
- I go swimming occasionally.
- Usually, we have dinner together.

## 3. Questions with Adverbs of Frequency

Use **How often** to ask about the frequency of an action.

Examples:

- How often do you exercise? — I exercise three times a week.
- How often does she visit her grandparents? — She visits them every weekend.
- How often do they play football? — They play football twice a week.

Common frequency expressions include:

| Expression | Example |
|---|---|
| Every day | I read every day. |
| Once a week | She goes swimming once a week. |
| Twice a month | We visit our grandparents twice a month. |
| Three times a year | They travel three times a year. |
| Every weekend | He plays tennis every weekend. |

**Grammar Tip:** Frequency expressions such as "every day", "once a week", and "twice a month" usually appear at the beginning or end of a sentence. They are not placed in exactly the same way as single-word adverbs such as "always" and "usually".

## 4. Common Grammar Mistakes

| Incorrect | Correct | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| I go always to school by bus. | I always go to school by bus. | Put the adverb before the main verb. |
| She always is happy. | She is always happy. | Put the adverb after "to be". |
| He doesn't never exercise. | He never exercises. | Avoid using two negative expressions together. |
| Do you always are tired? | Are you always tired? | Put "to be" before the subject in questions. |
| She go usually shopping. | She usually goes shopping. | Remember the third-person singular -s. |

## 5. Classroom Examples

Use these examples to help students talk about their daily routines.

- I always brush my teeth in the morning.
- I usually have breakfast at home.
- I often listen to music.
- I sometimes play video games.
- I rarely eat fast food.
- I never go to bed after midnight.

Encourage students to replace the activities with their own daily habits.

## 6. Classroom Activities

### Activity 1: Find Someone Who...

**Objective:** Practice asking and answering questions about habits.

**Instructions:**

1. Give students a list of activities.
2. Students walk around the classroom and ask their classmates questions.
3. They write down the names of people whose habits match the statements.

Example questions:

- Do you always eat breakfast?
- Do you often play sports?
- Do you sometimes cook dinner?
- Do you ever watch English movies?

Students can report their findings:

- Anna always eats breakfast.
- David sometimes cooks dinner.

### Activity 2: Frequency Line

**Objective:** Understand the meaning and order of adverbs of frequency.

**Instructions:**

1. Write "Always" on one side of the classroom and "Never" on the other.
2. Give students different adverbs of frequency.
3. Ask them to stand in the correct position along the line.
4. Invite students to explain their choices.

Suggested words:

Always, Usually, Often, Sometimes, Occasionally, Rarely, Hardly Ever, Never.

### Activity 3: My Weekly Habits

**Objective:** Practice writing sentences using adverbs of frequency.

Ask students to write five sentences about their weekly habits.

Example:

1. I always do my homework.
2. I usually help my parents.
3. I often play football.
4. I sometimes watch cartoons.
5. I never go to school on Sundays.

Students can share their sentences with a partner.

## 7. Grammar Practice

Choose the correct answer.

**1. I ______ brush my teeth before going to bed.**

- A. always
- B. am always
- C. always am
- D. be always

**2. She is ______ late for school.**

- A. never
- B. never is
- C. does never
- D. not never

**3. How often ______ you play tennis?**

- A. are
- B. does
- C. do
- D. is

**4. He ______ watches TV in the morning.**

- A. don't usually
- B. doesn't usually
- C. usually doesn't watches
- D. doesn't watches usually

**5. They visit their grandparents ______.**

- A. twice a week
- B. twice weeks
- C. twice in week
- D. two time a week

**6. My brother ______ gets up early because he starts work at 7 a.m.**

- A. usually
- B. is usually
- C. usually is
- D. does usually

**7. We ______ eat at restaurants because we prefer cooking at home.**

- A. hardly ever
- B. are hardly ever
- C. hardly never
- D. don't hardly ever

**8. Which sentence is correct?**

- A. She goes always swimming.
- B. She always goes swimming.
- C. Always she goes swimming.
- D. She go always swimming.

### Answer Key

1. A — always
2. A — never
3. C — do
4. B — doesn't usually
5. A — twice a week
6. A — usually
7. A — hardly ever
8. B — She always goes swimming.

## 8. Teaching Tips

- Use a frequency scale from 0% to 100% to explain the meaning of each adverb.
- Ask students to give examples based on their real daily routines.
- Use pictures of everyday activities to help students create sentences.
- Practice the difference between adverb placement with main verbs and the verb "to be".
- Encourage pair work so students can practice asking "How often...?" questions.
- Use short quizzes and sentence-building games to reinforce grammar patterns.
