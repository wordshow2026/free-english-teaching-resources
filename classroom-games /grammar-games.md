
# 10 Fun Grammar Games for English Classrooms

Grammar games help students understand and practice English grammar in an engaging and interactive way. Instead of simply memorizing rules, students can use grammar structures to communicate, solve problems, and work together.

This guide introduces 10 practical grammar games for English teachers. The activities require little preparation and can be adapted for different ages and English proficiency levels.

## 1. Sentence Scramble

**Objective:** Practice word order and sentence structure.

**Materials:** Word cards or prepared sentences.

**How to Play:**

1. Prepare a sentence and separate it into individual word cards.
2. Mix the cards and give them to students.
3. Students work individually or in groups to arrange the words into a correct sentence.
4. The first student or team to create the correct sentence wins.

**Example:**

Mixed words:

`every / plays / football / Tom / Sunday`

Correct sentence:

"Tom plays football every Sunday."

**Teaching Tips:**

- Start with short sentences for beginner learners.
- Include questions and negative sentences for extra practice.
- Ask students to explain why the word order is correct.

## 2. Grammar Auction

**Objective:** Identify grammatical errors and understand correct sentence structures.

**Materials:** A list of correct and incorrect sentences and pretend money.

**How to Play:**

1. Divide students into small teams.
2. Give each team the same amount of pretend money.
3. Present sentences for students to examine.
4. Teams bid on sentences they believe are grammatically correct.
5. Reveal the answers and award points based on correct purchases.

**Example sentences:**

| Sentence | Correct or Incorrect? |
|---|---|
| She goes to school every day. | Correct |
| He don't like coffee. | Incorrect |
| They are playing football. | Correct |
| I has a new book. | Incorrect |

**Teaching Tips:**

- Ask teams to correct the incorrect sentences.
- Use grammar structures students have already learned.
- Keep the bidding rules simple.

## 3. Sentence Building Challenge

**Objective:** Practice creating grammatically correct sentences.

**Materials:** Word lists or word cards.

**How to Play:**

1. Divide students into teams.
2. Give each team a set of words.
3. Ask them to create as many correct sentences as possible within a time limit.
4. Award points for correct grammar and meaningful sentences.

**Example words:**

`she / usually / breakfast / eat / at 7`

Possible sentence:

"She usually eats breakfast at 7."

**Teaching Tips:**

- Use vocabulary from previous lessons.
- Give extra points for creative but grammatically correct sentences.
- Ask students to identify the subject, verb, and other sentence elements.

## 4. Grammar Bingo

**Objective:** Recognize grammatical forms and reinforce grammar knowledge.

**Materials:** Bingo grids.

**How to Play:**

1. Give each student a bingo grid containing grammar examples.
2. Read a grammar clue or sentence aloud.
3. Students identify the matching item on their grids.
4. The first student to complete a row, column, or diagonal calls "Bingo!"
5. Check the answers before declaring a winner.

**Example:**

A grid might contain:

| Present Simple | Past Simple | Present Continuous |
|---|---|---|
| plays | played | is playing |
| watches | watched | are watching |
| studies | studied | am studying |

The teacher reads: "She is playing tennis now."

Students mark "is playing".

**Teaching Tips:**

- Use one grammar topic at a time.
- Ask students to explain the grammatical form they selected.
- Use sentence clues rather than isolated words for more advanced classes.

## 5. Find and Correct the Mistake

**Objective:** Develop grammar awareness and error-correction skills.

**Materials:** Prepared sentences containing grammatical errors.

**How to Play:**

1. Write several sentences with mistakes on the board.
2. Divide students into teams.
3. Teams take turns identifying and correcting errors.
4. Award points for each correct correction.
5. Discuss the grammar rules behind the mistakes.

**Example sentences:**

1. She go to school every day.
2. They was happy yesterday.
3. He doesn't likes pizza.
4. I am study English now.
5. There is three books on the table.

**Answers:**

1. She goes to school every day.
2. They were happy yesterday.
3. He doesn't like pizza.
4. I am studying English now.
5. There are three books on the table.

**Teaching Tips:**

- Focus on common mistakes made by your students.
- Ask students to explain the correction.
- Avoid introducing grammar rules that have not yet been taught.

## 6. Question Chain

**Objective:** Practice forming questions and giving appropriate answers.

**Materials:** None.

**How to Play:**

1. One student asks a question using a target grammar structure.
2. Another student answers and asks a new question.
3. Continue around the classroom.
4. Encourage students to use different subjects and question words.

**Example:**

- Student A: What do you usually eat for breakfast?
- Student B: I usually eat bread. What time do you get up?
- Student C: I get up at 6:30. Do you like English?
- Student D: Yes, I do. Where do you live?

**Teaching Tips:**

- Choose a grammar focus, such as present simple questions or past simple questions.
- Give students useful question starters.
- Encourage complete answers.

## 7. Grammar Charades

**Objective:** Practice recognizing and describing actions using grammatical structures.

**Materials:** Action cards.

**How to Play:**

1. Prepare cards with actions written on them.
2. One student picks a card and acts out the action without speaking.
3. Other students guess using a target sentence structure.
4. Award a point for a grammatically correct answer.

**Example:**

Target grammar: Present Continuous.

A student acts out swimming.

Possible answer:

"You are swimming!"

Other suggested actions:

- Reading a book
- Cooking dinner
- Playing basketball
- Brushing teeth
- Riding a bicycle

**Teaching Tips:**

- Use present continuous for actions happening now.
- Use past simple for actions that happened yesterday.
- Encourage students to form complete sentences.

## 8. Sentence Transformation Race

**Objective:** Practice changing sentences into different grammatical forms.

**Materials:** Prepared sentences and a board.

**How to Play:**

1. Divide students into teams.
2. Write a sentence on the board.
3. Give students a transformation instruction.
4. One student from each team writes the new sentence.
5. Check grammar and spelling.

**Example transformations:**

| Original Sentence | Instruction | Correct Answer |
|---|---|---|
| She likes coffee. | Make it negative. | She doesn't like coffee. |
| They are playing football. | Make it a question. | Are they playing football? |
| He visited London. | Make it negative. | He didn't visit London. |
| I am a student. | Change to "They". | They are students. |

**Teaching Tips:**

- Focus on one or two transformation types per round.
- Include affirmative, negative, and question forms.
- Ask students to explain changes to auxiliary verbs.

## 9. Grammar Story Challenge

**Objective:** Use grammar structures in meaningful writing and speaking.

**Materials:** Picture prompts or a list of words.

**How to Play:**

1. Divide students into small groups.
2. Give each group a topic, picture, or set of words.
3. Ask students to create a short story using a target grammar structure.
4. Groups present their stories to the class.
5. Award points for correct grammar, creativity, and clear communication.

**Example challenge:**

Target grammar: Past Simple.

Words:

`yesterday / park / friend / bicycle / rain`

Possible story:

"Yesterday, I went to the park with my friend. We rode our bicycles, but it started to rain. We went home quickly."

**Teaching Tips:**

- Provide sentence starters for beginner learners.
- Encourage more advanced students to use connecting words.
- Focus on communication as well as grammatical accuracy.

## 10. Multiple-Choice Grammar Quiz

**Objective:** Review grammar rules through short questions and challenges.

**Materials:** Prepared quiz questions or an interactive quiz.

**How to Play:**

1. Students play individually or in teams.
2. Present one multiple-choice question at a time.
3. Students select the correct answer.
4. Award points for correct answers.
5. Review the grammar rules behind difficult questions.

**Example questions:**

**Question 1:** She ______ to school every day.

- A. go
- B. goes
- C. going
- D. gone

Answer: B — goes.

**Question 2:** They ______ watching television now.

- A. is
- B. am
- C. are
- D. be

Answer: C — are.

**Question 3:** I ______ my homework yesterday.

- A. finish
- B. finishes
- C. finished
- D. finishing

Answer: C — finished.

**Teaching Tips:**

- Mix questions from previously learned grammar topics.
- Include explanations after each question.
- Use short quizzes as lesson warm-ups or revision activities.

## Choosing the Right Grammar Game

Different games help students practice different grammar skills.

| Learning Objective | Recommended Games |
|---|---|
| Sentence structure | Sentence Scramble, Sentence Building Challenge |
| Grammar recognition | Grammar Bingo, Multiple-Choice Grammar Quiz |
| Error correction | Grammar Auction, Find and Correct the Mistake |
| Question formation | Question Chain |
| Speaking practice | Grammar Charades, Grammar Story Challenge |
| Grammar transformations | Sentence Transformation Race |
| Writing practice | Grammar Story Challenge |

## Tips for Using Grammar Games Effectively

- **Choose one grammar focus:** Avoid combining too many unrelated rules in one activity.
- **Explain the rules first:** Students should understand the target structure before playing.
- **Demonstrate an example:** Show students how to play before starting.
- **Encourage teamwork:** Let students discuss their answers and explain their reasoning.
- **Provide useful feedback:** Explain why an answer is correct or incorrect.
- **Adapt the difficulty:** Use simple sentence patterns for beginners and more complex structures for advanced learners.
- **Review after the game:** Ask students to create their own examples using the target grammar.
